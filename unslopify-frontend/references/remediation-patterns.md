# Remediation Patterns

Use the smallest pattern that resolves the diagnosed root cause. Preserve the application’s established visual language when it is coherent.

## Remove Before Restyling

- Delete repeated subtitles, labels, and helper text.
- Merge duplicated state indicators.
- Remove decorative wrappers before changing their colors or radii.
- Remove low-value actions or move infrequent actions into a clear overflow menu.
- Replace generic sample content with a real next step or honest empty state.

## Flatten Without Losing Structure

- Replace nested cards with one meaningful surface and internal spacing.
- Use headings, alignment, and whitespace for subgroups.
- Keep a border only around interactive, selected, or semantically bounded content.
- Let the page background and one primary surface establish the main level.

## Restore Hierarchy

- Identify the primary task and primary action.
- Reduce emphasis on utilities, metadata, and advanced controls.
- Use a smaller type scale or lighter contrast for supporting information, not a second card.
- Vary spacing by hierarchy rather than applying one gap everywhere.

## Tighten Forms

- Keep labels concise and persistent.
- Use helper text only for surprising constraints or recovery.
- Put related fields in a simple grid or flow without per-field containers.
- Reveal conditional fields only after the controlling choice is selected.
- Place the submit action near the final field and resulting content.

## Reduce Pills And Badges

- Use plain inline text for ordinary metadata.
- Use tabs, links, checkboxes, or select controls according to actual behavior.
- Reserve badges for compact categorical or exceptional state.
- Avoid capsule styling on every filter, status, and action in the same region.

## Correct Action Density

- Keep one primary action per local task.
- Use familiar icon-only controls for repeated secondary actions when meaning is clear.
- Use an overflow menu for infrequent row actions.
- Keep destructive actions separated and explicitly labeled when consequences matter.
- Do not hide required or high-frequency actions merely to achieve visual cleanliness.

## Replace Explanation With Design

- Improve the label before adding helper text.
- Show expected format with a short example only when necessary.
- Reveal conditional controls at the moment they become relevant.
- Put feedback next to the changed content.
- Use a tooltip only for optional clarification, never for essential instructions.

## Remove AI And Trust Theater

- Replace generic “insight” cards with the actual evidence or recommendation.
- Remove confidence labels that lack calibrated meaning.
- Present generated content as content, with provenance or limitations where useful.
- Surface real failure, uncertainty, or stale state instead of reassuring decoration.

## Preserve Useful Complexity

- Keep dense controls when expert users need comparison or rapid operation.
- Keep boundaries around independent objects, destructive zones, or selection contexts.
- Keep domain terminology when it is precise and familiar to users.
- Keep status detail when it supports debugging, safety, or consequential decisions.

## Verify The Fix

- Compare before and after at the same viewport and state.
- Confirm the primary task is easier to locate and complete.
- Confirm state and recovery remain visible.
- Test keyboard, touch targets, responsive behavior, and reduced motion where relevant.
- Re-run the slop taxonomy against the result.
